# 1. What is this project actually?

Your assignment is to solve a business problem for NxtWave.

NxtWave wants to conduct a free online workshop called:

**"Build Your First AI Project in 60 Minutes."**

Your job is to figure out how to get **500 final-year engineering students to register for this workshop within 7 days, with a budget of just ₹2,000.**

You are not being asked to build an AI model or teach students how to create AI projects.

You are being asked to think like a **Growth Intern** who helps a company attract users and grow its audience.

For example, imagine NxtWave launches a new educational product. How would you make 500 students interested enough to register?

That is the problem you need to solve.

# 2. What exactly are we building?

I recommend building a project called:

## 🚀 AI LaunchPad — Student Growth Engine

It is a web application that helps NxtWave promote its workshop, collect student registrations, encourage referrals, and monitor the effectiveness of its marketing campaign.

Think of it as a small marketing platform with two sides.



### Part A: The student website

This is the website students see when they discover the workshop.

Imagine a student receives a WhatsApp message:

> Hey! Want to build your first AI project? Join this free 60-minute online workshop!

The student clicks the link and lands on your website.

The website displays:

**Build Your First AI Project in 60 Minutes**

- Free online workshop.
- Designed for engineering students.
- Beginner-friendly.
- Practical introduction to building an AI project.

There is a **Register Free** button.

The student clicks it, enters their details, and registers.

After registration, the student receives a confirmation and a unique referral link that they can share with friends.

That's the first part of your project.

### Part B: The admin dashboard

Now imagine you are the NxtWave growth team member responsible for getting 500 registrations.

You need to know:
- How many students have registered?
- How many more registrations do you need?
- Which colleges are bringing students?
- Are students registering through WhatsApp or referrals?
- Which marketing channel is performing best?
- Are you on track to reach 500 registrations within seven days?

Your dashboard answers these questions.

For example, imagine it displays:

| Metric | Example |
|---|---:|
| Registration target | 500 |
| Total registrations | 200 |
| Target completed | 40% |
| Registrations remaining | 300 |
| Days remaining | 3 |
| Required daily registrations | 100 |

These are hypothetical numbers for demonstrating the dashboard, not actual campaign results.

The dashboard can also display charts showing daily registrations, acquisition channels, and referral performance.

**This is the second part of your project.**

# 3. How does the complete project work?

Let's walk through a real-world example.

Imagine three students: Rahul, Priya, and Arjun.

### Step 1: Rahul discovers the workshop

Rahul sees a message in his college WhatsApp group.

He clicks the workshop link and visits your website.

### Step 2: Rahul registers

He enters his name, email address, college, graduation year, and branch.

The application saves his registration in the database.

The dashboard's registration count increases by one.

### Step 3: Rahul shares his referral link

After registering, Rahul receives a unique referral link.

He shares it with his friends.

### Step 4: Priya registers through Rahul's link

Priya opens Rahul's link and completes registration.

Your application records Priya's registration and attributes it to Rahul's referral code.

The dashboard now shows two registrations.

### Step 5: The growth team checks the dashboard

The team can see:
- Two registered students.
- One referral-attributed registration.
- The relevant acquisition sources.

### Step 6: The team makes a decision

Suppose the team notices that referrals are generating registrations, while another channel is generating many visits but very few registrations.

The team can investigate the weaker channel and improve its messaging or registration experience.

This is the important part: the application helps the team **measure growth and decide what to do next**.

# 4. Why do we need authentication?

You mentioned that you want authentication in your project.

Authentication is useful, but we should use it where it solves a real problem.

Your application will have two types of users.

| User | What they can do |
|---|---|
| Student | View the workshop, register, receive a referral link, and share it |
| Administrator | Log in, view campaign analytics, inspect registrations, and manage campaign settings |

Students do not need to create passwords before registering.

The administrator, however, must authenticate before accessing private campaign information.

You should also enforce administrator permissions in the backend and database, not just hide the dashboard from unauthorized users.

This gives you a real authentication feature without adding unnecessary friction to registration.

# 5. Why is this project relevant to a Growth Intern role?

Because it combines three important activities.

**1. Acquisition:** How do we attract students to the workshop?

You develop the marketing strategy using college clubs, student communities, referrals, and other relevant channels.

**2. Conversion:** How do we turn interested students into registered students?

You build a landing page and a simple registration process.

**3. Analytics and optimization:** How do we know what is working?

You build a dashboard that tracks registrations, referral attribution, channel performance, and progress toward the target.

Together, these form a basic growth system.

A landing page alone addresses only part of the problem. Your project connects the marketing strategy, registration process, and measurement system.

# 6. What makes your project unique?

I would add one feature that is particularly relevant to this assignment.

## The Growth Action Center

Instead of displaying only charts, the dashboard suggests what the growth team should investigate or do next.

For example:

**Situation:** The campaign has generated only 150 registrations by Day 4.

**Target:** 500 registrations in seven days.

**Dashboard insight:**
- Current registrations: 150.
- Remaining registrations: 350.
- Time remaining: 3 days.
- Required pace: approximately 117 registrations per day.

**Suggested action:**

"Registration pace is below target. Review the acquisition channels producing the most completed registrations, follow up with interested college partners, and test whether the landing page is converting visitors effectively."

The dashboard should use actual data to calculate the shortfall. The suggested action can initially be based on predefined rules rather than an expensive AI integration.

This demonstrates that you understand the difference between reporting data and using data to make decisions.
