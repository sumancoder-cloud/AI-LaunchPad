# 5. The growth strategy: how to aim for 500 registrations

Before building the dashboard, work out the acquisition plan.

Your budget is only ₹2,000, so paid advertising should not be the primary strategy. You need to prioritize distribution through student communities, clubs, and direct outreach.

Your plan should also distinguish **registration volume** from **attendance**. The assignment asks for registrations, so that is the primary target.

## Who exactly are you targeting?

### Primary audience

Final-year engineering students graduating in 2027, particularly students who:
- Want to strengthen their project portfolio.
- Are preparing for placements or internships.
- Are interested in AI but are unsure how to begin.
- Have learned programming but have not built an AI project.
- Want a practical introduction without committing to a long course.

This is a hypothesis about the audience's needs, not a claim that every student has these motivations.

### Secondary audience

- Student club coordinators.
- Technical society leads.
- College placement representatives, where outreach is appropriate.
- Class representatives and student community administrators.
- Students from earlier engineering years who may be interested, provided final-year students remain the priority.

### Why would they care?

Students may see value in a workshop that:
- Reduces the barrier to starting an AI project.
- Provides a structured first step rather than a long list of tutorials.
- Offers a practical activity they can discuss in interviews.
- Helps them understand what they would need to learn next.

Avoid promising that attending a one-hour workshop will guarantee an internship, placement, or job.

### What would make them register?

I would test three messages:

**Message A — Career relevance**

"Want to add an AI project to your portfolio but don't know where to start?"

**Message B — Low barrier to entry**

"Your first AI project. One guided hour. No AI experience required."

**Message C — Practical outcome**

"Stop collecting AI tutorials. Start building your first project."

Use these as hypotheses, not proven winning messages. Test them with small groups or compare their actual performance if the campaign were executed.

## The channel mix

I would prioritize four acquisition channels.

| Priority | Channel | Why it deserves attention |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | College clubs and student representatives | One relationship can distribute the workshop to many relevant students. |
| 2 | WhatsApp college and class groups | Students can share the workshop in communities they already use. |
| 3 | Student referrals | Registrants can bring in additional students through personal invitations. |
| 4 | LinkedIn and relevant student communities | Useful for extending reach beyond the initial college network. |

Email can support these channels, especially through approved student-club or community mailing lists. I would not assume that access to a large email database is available.

The campaign should not depend on scraping student contact details or sending unsolicited bulk messages.

## The seven-day execution plan

| Day | Primary activity | What you measure |
|---|---|---|
| Day 1 | Publish landing page, test registration and prepare campaign assets | Form completion rate and technical reliability |
| Day 2 | Contact student club coordinators and class representatives | Positive responses and distribution commitments |
| Day 3 | Launch WhatsApp distribution through approved groups and student networks | Registrations by source |
| Day 4 | Publish practical project-focused content and activate referrals | Referral visits and referred registrations |
| Day 5 | Follow up with interested clubs and underperforming channels | New registrations and channel conversion |
| Day 6 | Share a useful workshop preview and reminder through permitted channels | Registration momentum |
| Day 7 | Final reminder, fix any conversion issues and report campaign results | Total registrations and cost per registration |

Every channel should use a trackable campaign link or a clear source identifier.

For example:
- `?utm_source=college_club&utm_medium=community`
- `?utm_source=whatsapp&utm_medium=group`
- `?utm_source=student_referral&utm_medium=referral`
- `?utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=organic`

The application should preserve those source parameters through the registration flow.

Do not count a registration as attributable to a channel simply because the student visited that channel at some point. Define your attribution rule and apply it consistently.

## How could these channels produce 500 registrations?

This is the most important part of the marketing plan: show the arithmetic behind your target.

Use the following as a **planning model**, not a guarantee.

| Channel | Assumption | Registration target |
|---|---|---:|
| College clubs and student representatives | 20 active partners × 12 registrations each | 240 |
| WhatsApp and class communities | 10 permitted group distributions × 10 registrations each | 100 |
| Student referral loop | 100 registrations attributed to referral links | 100 |
| LinkedIn and student communities | 40 registrations | 40 |
| Permission-based email outreach | 20 registrations | 20 |
| **Total** | | **500** |

There is a potential overlap between these channels. For example, a student might see a WhatsApp post, visit LinkedIn, and then register using a friend's referral link.

For the plan to be credible, assign each registration to one primary source using a consistent attribution rule, deduplicate by student identity, and report unknown sources separately. The targets above assume that this is done.

### How I would validate the assumptions

Before claiming that this plan can reach 500 students, I would ask:

1. Can we realistically secure 20 student-community partners within two days?
2. Can each partner reach enough final-year students?
3. Are the WhatsApp groups relevant, active, and open to this kind of promotion?
4. How many referrals can reasonably come from the initial registrants?
5. Which channel produces registrations rather than just clicks?

If the first two assumptions fail, the plan needs to change. You cannot compensate for a lack of distribution simply by making the landing page more attractive.

### A better way to forecast

Let:

- \(P\) = active distribution partners.
- \(R_p\) = average registrations attributed to each partner.
- \(R_w\) = registrations from WhatsApp distributions.
- \(R_r\) = registrations from referrals.
- \(R_o\) = registrations from other channels.

Then:

\[
R_{\text{total}} = P \times R_p + R_w + R_r + R_o
\]

Your goal is:

\[
R_{\text{total}} \geq 500
\]

The dashboard should compare actual registrations against these assumptions daily.

If you are behind target, it should identify the shortfall and the channels most likely to help close it.

## Budget: ₹2,000

I would not spend the budget immediately.

| Expense | Budget | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| Small distribution or creative experiment | ₹800 | Test a limited promotional experiment if free distribution is insufficient |
| Contingency | ₹400 | Cover necessary campaign expenses |
| Reserve for the best-performing channel | ₹800 | Allocate after early evidence shows where the money is most useful |
| **Total** | **₹2,000** | |

The default should be organic distribution. Use existing design tools and student networks where possible, and release discretionary spending only after observing actual results.

If no paid promotion is necessary, preserve the budget rather than spending it just to use the allocation.

### One useful metric

\[
\text{Cost per registration} =
\frac{\text{Campaign spend}}{\text{Attributed registrations}}
\]

For example, if the entire ₹2,000 budget were spent to obtain 500 registrations:

\[
\text{Cost per registration} = \frac{2000}{500} = ₹4
\]

That is a budget scenario, **not a prediction that you will achieve ₹4 per registration**. Report the actual figure only after real spending and registrations have occurred.

# 9. AI + Learning Notes: how to score well on the reflection section

NxtWave explicitly asks what AI suggested, what you changed, and what you rejected.

Do not write generic statements such as "AI helped me save time." Give specific examples of decisions.

You need three examples, and they should describe your actual workflow. The following are examples of how to structure them. Adapt them to what genuinely happens while you build.

## Example 1 — Choosing the growth strategy

**What I asked AI**

"How can I get 500 final-year engineering students to register for a free AI workshop in seven days with ₹2,000?"

**What AI suggested**

A combination of social media promotion, college outreach, referrals and paid advertising.

**What I changed**

I prioritized college clubs, student representatives, permission-based WhatsApp distribution and referrals. I treated paid promotion as a limited experiment rather than the primary acquisition channel.

**Why**

The budget is small, and the target audience is concentrated in student communities. I wanted to maximize relevant distribution before spending money.

**What I learned**

A list of marketing channels is not a growth strategy. Each channel needs an audience, an expected contribution, a measurement method and a contingency plan.

## Example 2 — Reducing registration friction

**What I asked AI**

"Should students create accounts before they can register for the workshop?"

**What AI suggested**

An account-based experience with authentication before registration.

**What I changed**

I chose a guest registration flow and reserved authentication for the administrative dashboard.

**Why**

Requiring a password creates another step before the primary conversion. The workshop registration does not inherently require a student account.

**What I learned**

Authentication should solve a real access-control problem. It should not be added simply because a product needs to look technically advanced.

## Example 3 — Making analytics actionable

**What I asked AI**

"What analytics should I include in the growth dashboard?"

**What AI suggested**

Registration counts, charts, channel breakdowns and a referral leaderboard.

**What I changed**

I added a registration pace indicator, target gap, source-attribution rules and a Growth Action Center.

**Why**

A chart tells the team what happened. A useful growth dashboard should also help the team decide what to do next.

**What I learned**

Analytics becomes more valuable when it connects observed results to a specific action.
